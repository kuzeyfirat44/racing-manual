NASCAR Cup Series

America's
Stock Car Spectacle

Born on the beaches of Daytona, NASCAR is America's most-watched form of motorsport. Forty heavy, powerful stock cars race inches apart on banked ovals, superspeedways and road courses, where drafting, strategy and nerve decide the winner.

1948–1949
NASCAR Is Born
Bill France Sr. founds the National Association for Stock Car Auto Racing in Daytona Beach. In 1949 the first "Strictly Stock" race runs at Charlotte, the start of what is now the NASCAR Cup Series.
1959
Daytona & The First Daytona 500
Daytona International Speedway opens with its high-banked 2.5-mile tri-oval. The first Daytona 500 ends in a photo finish, and Lee Petty, Richard Petty's father, is declared the winner days later.
1970
The Winged Warriors
Dodge's Charger Daytona and Plymouth's Superbird bring nose cones and towering rear wings to stock car racing. Buddy Baker becomes the first driver to lap a closed course at over 200 mph. NASCAR restricts the winged cars from 1971.
1979
The Fight That Made NASCAR
The Daytona 500 is shown live from start to finish on national TV for the first time. A last-lap crash between leaders Cale Yarborough and Donnie Allison turns into a fistfight on the infield, Richard Petty wins, and millions of new fans tune in.
1987–1988
Too Fast: The Restrictor Plate Era
Bill Elliott qualifies at Talladega at 212.809 mph, still the fastest in NASCAR history. Days later, in the race itself, Bobby Allison's car rips into the catch fence. From 1988 NASCAR forces restrictor plates on cars at Daytona and Talladega to keep speeds down.
2001
Losing Dale Earnhardt
Seven-time champion Dale Earnhardt dies in a last-lap crash at the Daytona 500. NASCAR makes the HANS head-and-neck restraint mandatory that year and starts installing SAFER energy-absorbing barriers, changes that have saved countless drivers since.
2004
The Playoffs Arrive
NASCAR introduces "The Chase", a playoff format that decides the champion over the final races of the season. Jimmie Johnson masters it, winning five titles in a row from 2006 to 2010.
2022
The Next Gen Car
The seventh-generation Cup car debuts at the Daytona 500. It brings a sequential gearbox, independent rear suspension and 18-inch single-lug wheels, the biggest technical change in NASCAR's history.
2026
The Chase Returns
NASCAR drops the knockout playoff format it had used since 2014 and brings back the Chase. The top 16 drivers in the points battle it out over the final 10 races with no eliminations, and a race win is now worth 55 points.

The Kings of Stock Car

43
Richard Petty
United States
The King

Nobody will ever catch "The King". Richard Petty won 200 Cup races, almost twice as many as anyone else, and a record seven Daytona 500s. In 1967 alone he won 27 races, including ten in a row. With his No. 43, cowboy hat and sunglasses, he became the face of NASCAR for generations.

7× Cup Series Champion (1964, 1967, 1971, 1972, 1974, 1975, 1979)
200 Cup wins, the all-time record

3
Dale Earnhardt
United States
The Intimidator

The most feared driver NASCAR has ever seen. In his black No. 3 Chevrolet for Richard Childress Racing, Dale Earnhardt won seven titles with an aggressive style that earned him the nickname "The Intimidator". He finally won the Daytona 500 in 1998 on his 20th attempt. His death on the last lap of the 2001 Daytona 500 changed the sport's approach to safety forever.

7× Cup Series Champion (1980, 1986, 1987, 1990, 1991, 1993, 1994)
76 Cup wins
1998 Daytona 500 winner, on his 20th try
His death led to the mandatory HANS device
48
Jimmie Johnson
United States
Five-Time

The most dominant driver of the modern era. With Hendrick Motorsports and crew chief Chad Knaus, Jimmie Johnson won five championships in a row from 2006 to 2010, something no one else has done, and added two more to tie Petty and Earnhardt's record. He later raced in IndyCar and at Le Mans.

7× Cup Series Champion (2006–2010, 2013, 2016)
Record 5 consecutive championships
83 Cup wins
2× Daytona 500 winner (2006, 2013)

Legendary Stock Cars

Plymouth

Superbird

The car that brought Richard Petty back to Plymouth. With its pointed nose cone and huge rear wing, the Superbird was built for one thing: speed on NASCAR's superspeedways. Petty won 18 races in 1970, many of them in the Superbird. It was so effective that NASCAR restricted the winged cars from 1971.
