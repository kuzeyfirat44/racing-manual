Lewis Hamilton
United Kingdom
Modern Legend

Seven world championships, a shared record, and a record 106 Grand Prix wins. Lewis Hamilton redefined what it means to dominate Formula 1. From his title-winning breakthrough with McLaren to his long Mercedes era, he combines raw pace with tactical brilliance and tireless consistency. His 2020 title matched Michael Schumacher's record of seven. He joined Ferrari in 2025 and won his first race for them in Barcelona in 2026, aged 41.

7× F1 World Champion (2008, 2014, 2015, 2017, 2018, 2019, 2020)
106 Grand Prix wins, the all-time record (as of October 2026)
Most pole positions in F1 history
Six titles with Mercedes, including the dominant W11 in 2020
1
Michael Schumacher
Germany
Record Breaker

The man who rewrote the F1 record books. Michael Schumacher's five consecutive championships with Ferrari (2000–2004) represented total dominance and an era where the red cars were simply untouchable. His relentless work ethic, technical feedback, and obsessive pursuit of perfection transformed Ferrari into a dynasty. The Ferrari F2004 remains one of the most dominant F1 cars ever built.

7× F1 World Champion (1994, 1995, 2000, 2001, 2002, 2003, 2004)
91 race wins, the all-time record until 2020
Five consecutive titles with Ferrari
13 personal wins from 18 races in the 2004 Ferrari F2004
12
Ayrton Senna
Brazil
Immortal

Many consider Ayrton Senna the greatest racing driver who ever lived. His connection with the car bordered on supernatural, especially in the rain, where he was untouchable. Senna's battles with Prost, his blinding pace in Monaco, and his sheer commitment to perfection made him a legend. His tragic death at Imola in 1994 shook the world, but his legacy as F1's ultimate artist endures forever.

3× F1 World Champion (1988, 1990, 1991)
41 race wins, including the dominant MP4/4 season
Record 6 Monaco Grand Prix victories
65 pole positions, a mark of unmatched qualifying genius
14
Fernando Alonso
Spain
El Matador

Fernando Alonso is one of F1's most complete drivers: fast, intelligent, and utterly fearless. His back-to-back championships with Renault in 2005 and 2006 broke Ferrari and Schumacher's stranglehold on the sport. Alonso's racecraft and ability to extract maximum performance from any car made him a nightmare for rivals. Even in machinery that wasn't championship-worthy, he remained one of the grid's most dangerous drivers.

2× F1 World Champion (2005, 2006)
32 race wins and Renault R25 dominance
Defeated Michael Schumacher's Ferrari dynasty
Still racing in F1 in his forties with Aston Martin
